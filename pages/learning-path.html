Overview · Learning path

Learning path

109 types is a reference, not a syllabus. These 38 carry most of the work, arranged in three levels that build on one another — each assumes the one above it. Everything else in the dictionary is here for the day a problem calls for it.

The path in order

  1. 1System context diagram (C4 level 1)Who and what interacts with our system, and where does our responsibility end?
  2. 2Container diagram (C4 level 2)What are the separately deployable pieces, and how do they talk?
  3. 3Component diagram (C4 level 3)What is inside this one service, and what depends on what?
  4. 4Sequence diagramWhat calls what, in what order, and what happens when a step fails?
  5. 5Deployment diagramWhere does everything run, and what survives when part of it dies?
  6. 6Cloud architecture diagramHow do the AWS, Azure or GCP resources fit together, and what does each one guarantee?
  7. 7Data flow diagramHow does data move through the system, and where does it cross a boundary?
  8. 8Entity relationship diagramHow is data structured, and what are the real business rules about quantity?
  9. 9Domain modelHow is the domain structured, and where does consistency have to hold?
  10. 10State machine diagramWhat states exist, and which transitions are legal?
  11. 11OAuth 2.0 / OIDC flowHow does a caller prove who they are, and what does the token actually authorise?
  12. 12Network / VPC diagramWhat is reachable from where?
  13. 13Bounded context mapWhere should the seams between systems and teams fall?
  14. 14Event topology diagramIf I change this event's schema, who breaks?
  15. 15CQRS diagramHow do the write and read models differ, and how far apart can they drift?
  16. 16Saga diagramHow does a multi-service transaction complete, or unwind when a step fails?
  17. 17Integration architecture diagramHow do these systems talk, and how many integrations are we really maintaining?
  18. 18CI/CD pipeline diagramHow does a change reach production, and how does it come back out?
  19. 19Failover / DR diagramHow much data do we lose, and how long are we down?
  20. 20Observability architectureHow do we find out something broke, and then why?
  21. 21Swimlane diagramWhat are the steps, and who owns each one?
  22. 22Data lineage diagramWhere did this number come from?
  23. 23Quality attribute scenario modelWhat does “fast enough” actually mean here, and how would we know we missed it?
  24. 24Caching architectureWhere are the copies, how stale may they be, and what happens when the cache is cold?
  25. 25Migration & transition-state architectureHow do we get from this to that without a big-bang cutover?
  26. 26Event storming boardWhat actually happens in this business, in what order?
  27. 27Event sourcing diagramWhat is the system of record, and can we reconstruct any past state?
  28. 28Kafka topic architectureWhat is ordered relative to what, and how far can this scale out?
  29. 29Service mesh diagramHow is east-west traffic secured and controlled?
  30. 30Kubernetes architecture diagramHow does a workload actually get scheduled and reached in this cluster?
  31. 31Zero trust architectureWhy is a request from inside the network treated no differently from one outside it?
  32. 32Threat model (STRIDE)What can go wrong here, and what are we doing about each one?
  33. 33Fault tree analysisWhat combinations of failures produce this outcome?
  34. 34Reliability block diagramDoes this design actually reach the availability number we promised?
  35. 35Serverless architectureWhat triggers what, and what happens to the messages that fail?
  36. 36LLM application & agent architectureWhat can this agent actually do, how many times, and at what cost per request?
  37. 37IoT architectureHow do a hundred thousand intermittently connected devices stay useful and updatable?
  38. 38Multi-tenancy isolation modelHow isolated is one tenant from another, and what does that cost per tenant?
Levels  —  L1 foundation: describe any system. L2 core practice: design and operate a distributed one. L3 specialist: depth where the domain requires it. The learning path sequences 38 of the 109 types across those levels. Sample plates are drawn to one house style; they show the notation, not a real system.
